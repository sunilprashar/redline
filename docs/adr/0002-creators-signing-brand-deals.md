# 0002 — Creators signing brand deals

Status: amended by ADR-0010 · 2026-08-28

## Decision

Version one serves creators signing brand deals, reading contracts,
agreements, and platform terms — the documents that one segment actually
encounters. Residential leases are out of scope. This narrows the four
document types named in `CLAUDE.md`, which is amended to match.

## Alternatives

- **Freelancers.** The loudest evidence in the research: 62% lost wages to
  nonpayment. Rejected because that harm sits downstream of signing.
  Nonpayment is an enforcement failure, and reading the contract more
  carefully at signing does not prevent it.
- **Franchisees and founders.** Highest stakes and a proven four-figure spend
  — which is also evidence that a lawyer is the norm there, putting us closest
  to the lawyer-substitution claims the FTC sanctioned DoNotPay over.
- **Employees facing non-competes.** Strongest first-person evidence and the
  most sympathy. Rejected on economics: frightened and broke at the moment
  they need us.

## Why

Creator harm is a comprehension failure, which is the only kind a reading tool
prevents. A perpetual worldwide licence, a category exclusivity, a morality
clause — these are understood too late and are irreversible once signed.
Brand deals are also short enough to parse in the browser, signed often enough
to build a habit, and priced by lawyers at $2,850, the most expensive review
category found and one nobody in the segment is buying.

## Consequences

- Leases leave scope despite the research folder covering tenants. The
  evidence gap there is a crawler artifact, not a finding — so this is a bet on
  focus, not a claim that tenants are fine.
- The general pitch is gone. "Any contract you're about to sign" sells better
  than "brand deals"; we trade reach for being good at something.
- Platform terms are read but cannot be negotiated, so the counter-offer
  feature does not apply to them. Unresolved.
