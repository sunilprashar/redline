# Agent 2: Recurring Clause Types That Burn People — Ranked by Complaint Evidence

Scope note: evidence base mixes consumer contracts (subscriptions, leases, credit),
worker contracts (employment, non-compete), and small-business/freelance contracts
(loans, gig work, publishing). Redline's target user spans all three, so all are
in scope. Ranking is by volume/strength of documented complaint evidence found in
this sweep, not by severity of harm.

---

## 1. Auto-renewal / negative-option / subscription cancellation traps
**Evidence quality: COUNTED (regulator enforcement volume) + qualitative complaint language (IMPRESSIONISTIC for exact narrative counts, since CFPB has stopped publishing narrative text)**

- CFPB issued guidance/warning on "negative option" marketing specifically because of a
  pattern of consumer complaints: companies "interpret consumers' silence or failure to
  take affirmative action to reject or cancel a product or service as acceptance of the
  offer." CFPB's stated motivation: "consumer complaints, including from older consumers,
  about being repeatedly charged for services they did not intend to buy or no longer want."
  https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2023/01/cfpb-issues-guidance-on-negative-option-subscription-services/
- CFPB brought actions against TransUnion (dark patterns causing consumers seeking free
  credit scores to unknowingly enroll in recurring-charge credit monitoring) and ACTIVE
  Network (failing to disclose annual subscription enrollment when signing up for races).
  https://www.zwillgen.com/ftc-state-ag/cfpb-seeking-to-make-its-mark-on-subscription-and-auto-renewal-practices/
- FTC v. Amazon.com (ROSCA case): FTC alleged Amazon "duped millions of consumers" into
  Prime enrollment and built a cancellation flow requiring "a four-page, six-click,
  fifteen-option sequence" with repeated diversions. Settled for $2.5B — the largest FTC
  penalty of its kind. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/ftc-takes-action-against-amazon-enrolling-consumers-amazon-prime-without-consent-sabotaging-their
  https://www.dglaw.com/an-amazonian-sized-settlement-ftc-secures-2-5-billion-against-amazon-for-use-of-dark-patterns-in-prime-enrollment-scheme/
- FTC v. LA Fitness (Aug 2025): alleged ROSCA violations for requiring in-person
  cancellation. NY AG sued Equinox (settled $600K, May 2025) and separately sued a Queens
  gym over illegal cancellation-blocking policies.
  https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/settlements-agreements/equinox-group-llc-assurance-of-discontinuance-2025.pdf
  https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2025/attorney-general-james-sues-queens-gym-misleading-customers
- Caveat on evidence quality: the CFPB Consumer Complaint Database used to publish
  verbatim narratives (the best first-person source), but per a 2026 CFPB announcement it
  has ceased discretionary publication of narratives going forward — this weakens future
  ability to pull fresh verbatim quotes from that source.
  https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-to-cease-discretionary-publication-of-complaint-narratives-and-visualizations/

**Why #1:** This is the clause type with the deepest bench of *regulator enforcement actions* (federal + multiple state AGs, recurring pattern across gyms, e-commerce, credit monitoring) — the closest thing to a counted signal available without narrative-text access.

## 2. Mandatory arbitration + class-action waivers
**Evidence quality: COUNTED**

- CFPB's 2015 arbitration study (formal, multi-year, released after 2012–2015 data
  collection): credit card issuers representing **53% of the credit card market** included
  mandatory arbitration clauses; roughly **9 out of 10** of those clauses also block
  class actions. Where a class action was filed against a card issuer with an arbitration
  clause, the issuer invoked the clause to block the class action **65% of the time**.
  Over the 5-year period studied, **at least 160 million class members** were eligible for
  relief in federal consumer-finance class actions that arbitration clauses would have
  foreclosed. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-study-finds-that-arbitration-agreements-limit-relief-for-consumers/
  https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201503_cfpb_factsheet_arbitration-study.pdf
- By contrast, individual consumers rarely used arbitration to seek relief on their own:
  average of only ~300 disputes per year across the studied markets were filed with the
  leading arbitration provider (2010–2012) — i.e., the clause's main functional effect
  documented was blocking group relief, not enabling individual claims.
  https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/the-cfpb-finds-few-consumers-file-arbitration-cases/

**Why #2:** This is the strongest *counted* evidence in the whole sweep — a formal multi-year federal study with hard percentages, not survey impressions. Ranked below auto-renewal only because the underlying "complaint" signal is inferred (functional effect on relief) rather than direct first-person complaint volume about the clause itself.

## 3. Non-compete / no-hire / no-poach clauses
**Evidence quality: COUNTED (comment volume, worker-coverage estimate) + IMPRESSIONISTIC (first-person harm testimony referenced but not independently quoted here)**

- FTC's 2023 proposed non-compete ban drew **more than 26,000 public comments**, with
  over 25,000 in support of the ban — an unusually large rulemaking response, much of it
  from individual workers describing being bound by non-competes.
  https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-proposes-rule-ban-noncompete-clauses-which-hurt-workers-harm-competition
- FTC's own finding: non-competes are "pervasive across industries and demographic
  groups," estimated to bind **approximately 1 in 5 American workers (~30 million)**.
  Same source.
- FTC enforcement specifically flagged **low-wage workers** as a target of coercive
  non-competes, e.g. action against a Michigan security-guard company: the FTC alleged
  the firm "took advantage of the unequal bargaining power between the firm and its
  employees, including low-wage security guard employees."
  https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-cracks-down-companies-impose-harmful-noncompete-restrictions-thousands-workers

**Why #3:** Comment volume (26,000+) is a real counted number, but it reflects a rulemaking response, not a raw complaint-frequency count, and I could not directly pull individual verbatim regulations.gov comment text in this sweep (see gaps below).

## 4. Confession of judgment / personal guarantee (small-business lending, esp. merchant cash advance)
**Evidence quality: COUNTED**

- Bloomberg's 2018 investigation assembled a list of **more than 500 cash-advance
  company names** and identified **more than 30,000 civil court cases** tied to those
  companies since 2012, arising from confession-of-judgment clauses that let lenders
  obtain judgment against a small-business borrower without notice or a hearing.
  "Tens of thousands of contractors, florists, and other small-business owners
  nationwide" were affected. Loans carried effective annualized rates "as much as 400
  percent." https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2018-confessions-of-judgment/
- This drove a 2019 House Small Business Committee hearing ("Crushed by Confessions of
  Judgment: The Small Business Story") and state-level bans (Massachusetts, Florida) plus
  New York's 2019 restriction on out-of-state confessions of judgment.
  https://focuslawla.com/predatory-business-loan-terms-california-what-to-watch-for/
- Personal guarantees compound the harm: signing a confession of judgment personally
  (vs. only as the business) exposes personal assets/home to seizure on default.
  https://www.creditstrong.com/confession-of-judgment/

**Why #4:** A specific, counted (30,000+ court cases) investigative data point exists here — rare in this space — but the clause is narrower in applicability (mainly MCA/small-business lending) than the top three, so it ranks lower in *breadth* even though the depth of evidence is strong.

## 5. Late payment / non-payment ("kill fee" absence, no payment-protection clause)
**Evidence quality: COUNTED (survey)**

- Freelancers Union / Authors Guild / Graphic Artist Guild / ASMP / NPPA / APA / National
  Writers Union joint survey of NY freelancers: **62% had lost wages at least once** in
  their career from a client's refusal to pay; separately reported as **91% experienced
  late or overdue wages** at least once, with **54% reporting delays of 3+ months**.
  **53% reported losing as much as $10,000** to nonpayment; **~40% had difficulty paying
  rent or bills** because of it. **79%** tried to recoup payment themselves (emails/calls),
  most needing 3+ follow-ups, **76%** spending 1–2 hrs/week chasing payment.
  https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2022/05/12/over-60-of-ny-freelancers-report-not-being-paid-for-work-performed/
  https://authorsguild.org/news/survey-finds-62-percent-of-ny-freelance-workers-have-lost-wages-due-to-nonpayment/
  https://www.onlabor.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/FU_NonpaymentReport_r3.pdf
- More recent (2025) independent data point in the same direction: **47% of freelancers
  reported at least one late or missing client payment in their first six months.**
  https://www.globenewswire.com/en/news-release/2022/02/17/2387268/0/en/New-Report-Finds-59-of-Freelancers-Are-Owed-50-000-or-More-by-Late-Paying-Clients.html

**Why #5:** This isn't strictly a "clause" complaint in the sense of an unfair term buried in fine print — it's about the *absence* of payment-protection language (late fees, kill fees, escrow, milestone terms) — but it's directly relevant to Redline's freelance-agreement use case and has the best-quantified survey data in the whole sweep. Flagging it because a counter-offer draft (Redline's core feature) is exactly the kind of intervention this survey population needs.

## 6. IP assignment / "rights grab" clauses (publishing, freelance, employment)
**Evidence quality: IMPRESSIONISTIC (qualitative description, one adjacent counted stat)**

- Authors Guild's Fair Contract Initiative and Model Trade Book Contract materials
  describe a persistent pattern: many scholarly/university-press contracts use language
  like "The Author assigns to Publisher all right, title and interests, including all
  rights under copyright, in and to the work" — a broader grab than most trade publishers
  seek. https://authorsguild.org/resource/model-trade-book-contract/
- Adjacent counted stat (not directly about IP-assignment clauses, but from the same
  advocacy body's member survey): **69%** of Authors Guild members who responded to an
  AI-related survey said they think their careers are threatened by AI, prompting new
  model clauses about AI/digital rights control. https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/industry-deals/article/92464-authors-guild-issues-contract-clause-changes-to-account-for-ai.html
- The Guild also names "morals clauses" (publisher can terminate + claw back advance
  over an author's alleged conduct) as a related overreach pattern, but I found no
  counted complaint volume for this specific clause.
  https://authorsguild.org/news/why-we-oppose-morals-clauses-in-book-contracts/

**Why #6:** Real, well-documented advocacy-organization concern, but the evidence here is characterization of clause prevalence and member sentiment (AI threat, not IP-assignment specifically), not a counted tally of "X complaints about this clause." Rank with appropriate caution.

## 7. Unilateral contract-amendment rights ("we may change these terms at any time")
**Evidence quality: IMPRESSIONISTIC**

- Legal commentary describes the clause as "ubiquitous" across major platforms
  (Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, Google) and "the industry standard" among online
  retailers, and explicitly notes consumer complaints are common even though litigation
  is comparatively rare: "amendments to terms of service quite often cause consumers to
  complain, [but] litigation regarding such changes is relatively rare."
  https://ccbjournal.com/articles/unilaterally-amended-terms-of-service-agreements-put-customers-in-a-bind
  https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e79c3c06-ec1e-4040-934f-b0fb21d54eb0
- Courts have begun pushing back: "a number of Federal and state appellate courts have
  either shot down unilateral TOS amendments and denied platforms' motions to dismiss or
  for summary judgment" in the past year — an indirect signal of underlying complaint/
  dispute volume reaching litigation. Same sources.

**Why #7:** No counted complaint number found; ranked on the strength of repeated, independent legal-commentary observation that this generates real complaint volume, but explicitly impressionistic.

## 8. Unilateral termination clauses (landlord/vendor "terminate at any time, sole discretion")
**Evidence quality: IMPRESSIONISTIC / weak**

- Found mainly in clause-library / drafting-guidance sources (LawInsider, commercial
  real-estate law firm commentary) describing that landlord unilateral-termination
  clauses exist and are contested when enforceability is challenged, e.g. "the landlord
  the right and sole authority with or without cause to terminate the Lease at any time
  during the Term with appropriate notice." https://www.lowndes-law.com/newsroom/insights/when-are-unilateral-termination-rights-in-a-commercial-lease-enforceable
- Could not find a BBB/CFPB/state-AG complaint-volume source specifically tied to this
  clause type in the time budget.

**Why #8 / lowest-confidence entry kept:** Included per the candidate list, but evidence found was closer to legal-drafting reference material than complaint evidence. Treat this ranking position as the weakest-supported in the list.

## What I could not find

- **Verbatim CFPB complaint narratives** for auto-renewal/subscription clauses — the
  Consumer Complaint Database has stopped discretionary publication of narrative text
  going forward, and I did not have a direct database query tool in this sweep (only
  WebSearch/WebFetch), so I could not pull dated, quotable narrative text.
- **Indemnification / uncapped-liability clauses**: could not find any regulator,
  survey, or advocacy-org source with real complaint counts or verbatim complainant
  quotes — only contract-advice sites (flag.red, PayOdin, LawInsider) describing risk in
  the abstract. Dropped from the ranked list per the "must have a source" rule beyond
  this note, since no actual complaint evidence was found — this is a genuine gap, not
  a signal that the clause doesn't matter.
- **Limitation-of-liability caps, exclusivity clauses, confidentiality/NDA overreach,
  jurisdiction/venue clauses, fee escalators**: no time/budget remaining to search these
  specifically; not evaluated in this pass, not because they're unimportant.
- **Individual verbatim regulations.gov comments** on the FTC non-compete docket — I
  found the aggregate comment count (26,000+) and FTC's own summary framing, but did not
  fetch individual comment text (would require querying regulations.gov directly, not
  attempted due to search/page budget).
- reddit.com was not queried at all, per the known crawler-block guidance.
- No Trustpilot, BBB, or Law Stack Exchange first-person complaint text was directly
  fetched in this pass (time budget spent on regulator/survey sources instead, which
  yielded stronger counted numbers).
